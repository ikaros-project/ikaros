## Development Notes

The week opened by closing out a long kernel refactoring run. Several core implementation areas were moved into dedicated translation units, public field access was audited and reduced, declarations were split into more focused headers, and a range of small internal helpers around metadata, diagnostics, parsing, persistence, and connection handling were tightened or removed. That did not change the external story of Ikaros so much as it made the kernel boundary cleaner and more explicit by the end of Monday.

The center of gravity then moved decisively into the WebUI and BrainStudio surface. A large sequence of commits standardized widget parameter naming, shared more of the control and drawing framework, aligned graph and layout controls, and made edit-mode behavior less brittle across controls, sliders, menus, world views, and canvas widgets. Many of the follow-up commits were small edge-case fixes, but together they made the widget layer behave more like one coherent system rather than a collection of special cases.

That cleanup also created room for a visible expansion of the visualization set. `World2DView` and `World3DView` were normalized into the selector, `SurfacePlot`, `PolarPlot`, and `VectorField` received dedicated BrainStudio widgets and examples, and the HUD work grew into navigation and pan-tilt overlays with corrected degree rendering. By Thursday, `Trace`, `EventRaster`, and `HeatMap` had joined the same push with gallery examples and presentation refinements, so the week broadened not just the safety of existing widgets but also the kinds of data BrainStudio could present directly.

Runtime behavior was adjusted in parallel with the presentation work. WebUI image encoding was moved outside the kernel tick, slow-opening views gained clearer progress reporting, stopped systems reloaded before play, and fast-forward execution gained live WebUI updates with follow-up fixes for responsiveness, tick delivery, and step-to-realtime clock alignment. By the end of the week, the runtime and browser-side surface were more synchronized: richer widgets were available, and the execution loop exposed them with less lag and fewer stale-state problems.
